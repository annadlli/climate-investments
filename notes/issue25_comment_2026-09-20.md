ICC write-up and buyout test are in Dropbox `Flooding/Notes/icc_2026-09-20.md`. Short version:

**What ICC is.** Coverage D of the SFIP: up to $30,000 (nominal) to bring a substantially or repetitively damaged building into compliance, paid only after the community's floodplain administrator makes the determination and the work passes inspection. Eligible measures are elevation, relocation, demolition, and (non-residential only) floodproofing. ICC can fund the non-federal share of an HMGP/FMA elevation and can pay for demolition ahead of a buyout, so a payment is evidence of one of four outcomes, two of which remove the house. In the claims file the payment sits on the triggering flood's record (`yearOfLoss`), so `nfip_icc` is the flood year, not the completion year.

**Current screen.** `complete.do` counts ICC as an elevation only if the property has no Builty permit or NFIP flag flip and keeps its policy after the ICC year.

**Test on the panel (FL LA TX, 2,618 ICC properties).** 43% leave the panel in the ICC year and are screened out. Among the 57% that stay, the NFIP elevated flag flips after the payment for 0.7%; 48% were already flagged elevated before the flood and 51% never show as elevated. County-level HMA acquisition exposure is above 80% in LA and TX for stayers and leavers alike, so it cannot separate buyouts from lapses.

**Proposal.** Keep ICC as `elevation_source` 2 but describe it in the appendix as uncorroborated; the event studies already use Builty permits only. Replace the continued-coverage rule with a property-level buyout check when the GOHSEP and RI lists arrive, or in the meantime via ATTOM (transfer to a public grantee or demolition permit within two years of the ICC year). One thing to settle first: whether `elevated` in the policy file is a per-year characteristic or the current rating carried across years. If the latter, the NFIP flag flips (source 1) are re-ratings, not elevations.

**Added later the same day.** Two more screens: funded acquisitions in the property's own ZIP within three years (OpenFEMA mitigated properties, the NPR buyout source) hit 37% of stayers and 37% of leavers; on the assigned ATTOM property, improvement value falls to zero for about 4% of both groups and nominal transfers are 0-3%. So nothing observable separates the 43% that drop coverage from the rest, and the continued-coverage rule stays as the only screen, unvalidated, until a property-level list arrives.

**09-21, final.** The data-appendix paragraph with sources (FEMA ICC page and fact sheet, WYO Bulletin W-15038, Kousky and Lingle 2017) is at the end of `notes/icc_2026-09-20.md`. Two facts worth having: nationally 62% of ICC money goes to elevation and 30% to demolition, and 99% of claims are substantial-damage determinations. On our SFHA sample 2,183 homes got an ICC payment, 1,259 enter the harmonized variable as ICC elevations and 887 (41%) are screened out by the continued-coverage rule, which is about the demolition share plus ordinary lapse. So the rule removes roughly the right number but cannot identify which homes; the NFIP flag, ZIP-level acquisitions, the NPR buyout list (matched through ATTOM) and the ATTOM record after the ICC year all fail to separate them, because the NFIP-ATTOM link is a cell proxy. Proposal stands: keep ICC as source 2, report it as uncorroborated, event studies on Builty permits only.
